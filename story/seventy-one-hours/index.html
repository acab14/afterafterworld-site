Seventy-One Hours

The first Lieutenant dies. Everything it cost, and the clock that started the moment it fell.

Where we are: Innominandum is dead — the first of Ghroth’s three Lieutenants ever to fall. The Feywild is whole. The party is back in Wip with a long rest, twelve hours of quiet, and a countdown they cannot pause.

The last root

They cut it — and Innominandum came down out of the canopy onto Marvin’s seal directly, and raised a titan out of the beauty it had stolen: one last thing standing between the party and the door it was eating.

It was driven off anyway. And then it ran, the way its kind always runs: through a hole in the world, into the Far Realm, where nothing that could kill it was supposed to be able to follow.

The party followed it.

What they gave up at the threshold

Divine things do not cross. So at the edge of the portal the party set them down in a pile on the grass:

  • Una put down her entire dragonhood — Kereska’s consecration, Freya’s aspect, Etta the falcon — eleven hours after she had been forged into it, and walked into hell as something less.
  • Asfache left the green orb, and watched Cal dissolve in front of him for the second time in his life, knowingly, and went in anyway.
  • Rat lost Thoth entirely. The man who built a life out of hearing what other people can’t, walking into a place where everything was talking and none of it was language.
  • R4 lost Aditi, and with her the ability to touch another mind — alone inside his own head for the first time since the day he woke up.
  • Steven had already lost his goddess. What the Far Realm gave him instead was that his Siren Song teeth got stronger there. The one part of him Essyliss punished is the part that place feeds.
  • Smolder went bald.

Everything divine, left on the grass. They walked in as themselves and nothing else.

Six of them crossed: Rat, R4, Steven, Smolder, Una, Asfache.

The Wrong Garden

On the other side was a garden built out of everything Innominandum had eaten — beautiful, wrong, and running on machinery that had no business existing in a place like that.

Sofia was inside it.

Beautiful, and running on gears. She was hanging in the middle of it with her eyes open.

She had gone into Innominandum as an air elemental and could not get out, and it had found a use for her. The frog sac Ghroth’s Siren Song put in her throat during the First Run turned out to be a receiver. Innominandum hollowed her out and put its network in the empty space, and used her to talk to the other two.

Una and Rat flew in and pulled her out.

She came out breathing. What it cost her, before they reached her, bought most of what the party now knows: where Tenebrosum is and how it feeds, what Mappo No Ryujin looks like, how much of the Pool of Memories is left — and the one genuine gift in all of it, that Mappo is anchored to the Pool and cannot leave it. Whenever the party goes, it will be waiting exactly there. They choose the moment.

She has not woken since, and she will not.

The governorless engine

Six hands on the tendrils — every one of them chose to stay.

Losing, Innominandum reached into its own engine and tore out the governor — choosing to detonate the realm rather than be killed in it.

Asfache saw the tendrils still trailing off the torn-out controls and called everyone onto them. R4 had the Amulet of the Planes in his hand and an exit for all of them, and did not use it. And the whole party took hold at once and became the governor themselves — six pairs of hands forcing a limit onto a machine that had none.

The engine slowed. Innominandum, with nothing left to root in and nowhere to hide, materialized inside its own works.

Smolder and Rat killed it there.

Seventy years

Thoth, Kratos, and Freya watch Thoril hold the dome.

Marvin’s seal finished the fight at full strength — not cracked, not scarred, not leaking. Whole.

That did not happen on its own.

During the fight, while everyone else was busy, R4 poured seventy years of his Farylse time into the dome. Seventy years of the time the party carried out of a plane they can never return to, spent quietly into a golden shell so a dead man could keep holding a door.

That is why the Feywild recovers whole instead of carrying grey scars forever. That is why Aquandria pledged the entire fey host to the Edge of Existence — every court, unconditionally, the largest alliance win of the Second Run. And that is why Marvin got a last lucid conversation before he settled permanently into the seal.

Nobody thanked him. Nobody has done the arithmetic yet.

What it cost

Sofia. Her throat is open along its full length and breathes on its own. Her eyes do not close. Her pulse and her breathing run at two different speeds. Sometimes she says things in a language nobody can read, to nobody at all. Nothing fixes this — not the Oakfather, not restoration, not wishes.

Mielikki came to the grove, stopped at the edge, said nothing, and wept. Eldath asked permission to come and sit with her once a week. The Oakfather did not come.

Horus and Nubia, spent to keep the party standing. Only Bast is left.

Half of Thoril, with the other half already owed to Callahan’s door.

And where Innominandum was rooted there is a circle of grey ground two hundred feet across where nothing grows. The fey are calling it The Silence, and they will still be calling it that in a thousand years.

The modron

Pentad-Nine, riding out of the Far Realm on the one who chooses hardest.

Something else walked out of the wreckage.

Pentad-Nine was the engine’s limiter — a Mechanus pentadrone that Innominandum tore out of its governor-seat and fed into the gears. It survived. It watched six creatures that are not supposed to be predictable choose to take the governor’s place, with an exit available and declined, and it concluded that this was impossible and had happened anyway.

Its verdict: “the proof is incomplete” — the proof being the one Primus accepted from outside Mechanus, that Ghroth’s end is inevitable.

It attached itself to Asfache — the one who found the tendrils and the one carrying it — and it rides on his back now. It is never wrong. It is never kind. It never volunteers anything.

Which mattered, because when somebody finally thought to ask it how long they had, it answered immediately. It had been holding the number the entire time.

Seventy-one hours until Tenebrosum finishes Callahan. The moment Innominandum died, Tenebrosum doubled its feeding to make up the loss.

It knows when. It does not know why, and it says so.

Twelve hours in Wip

They could not walk at a Lieutenant like that. Casters dry, no long rest since before the Far Realm, six of them where there used to be nine. Resting in a convalescent Feywild was a gamble on time itself.

So the gods carried them home to Wip — and then went their own ways.

Thoth, Kratos, and Freya returned to the Edge of Existence. They will come to the Shadowfell the way they came to the Feywild: when Thoril calls them.

Thoril went ahead to Mystica, alone, at half of what he was.

Eldath carried Sofia to LIMA.

And for twelve hours, the world got quiet.

Una gives away a piece of herself

Her father is still standing in a field in the Outlands. A creator god was mid-sentence when Thoth tore them out of the Dragonsmoot, and Una will not get back there inside seventy-one hours.

So she found a magic item maker in Wip, handed over one of her own golden dragon scales, and commissioned a communication device out of it — to give Oman the next time she sees him, so that this never happens again.

She has not finished it. She still has to reach him.

Asfache buys back fifteen years

In the timeline that no longer exists, he hatched Rodney and spent years turning him into a mount. The reset put the griffon back in the egg, and there was never going to be time to do it twice.

So he spent fifteen years of his own Farylse time, and raised him in an afternoon.

Rodney is a warmount now, in his prime, with decades in front of him. The fifteen years are gone. Farylse is closed forever.

The rider got some work done too: R4 spent part of the same twelve hours reworking Asfache’s mechanical leg into a griffon’s.

Steven sees someone leave

He went to LIMA with Rat to sit with Sofia.

There was an elf in white across the room, and she left the instant she saw him.

He went after her and lost her. So he did the other thing — he asked around his own organization, the one he built, and came away with a name.

Sienna.

An elf volunteer at a lizardfolk aid house does not run from the man who founded it. Not unless she knows exactly who he is.

Rat stays

He was one of the two people who reached into Innominandum and pulled Sofia out.

He sat down beside her and did not leave.

She talks constantly, in something that is not a language. Thoth’s one gift to Rat is that he understands any language that is spoken. He has not mentioned it to anyone.

R4 blows up the Catty Shack

Smolder spent his one day off complaining about the competition.

R4 listened, excused himself, went and got a La Bomba, and leveled the building.

Where that leaves them

Somewhere with no light in it at all: the Sunless Lake.

And on the Astral, something drinks the Pool of Memories smaller.

Roughly fifty-nine hours.

Alamil’s circles can put them on Mystica within the hour — or so everyone believes this morning — and Catroniel is already there with Junish airships and a resistance army. From there: Albion, where a mad queen guards a Shadowfell portal and does not remember why. Then down into the deep Shadowfell, to a dry lakebed where light has never reached — the Sunless Lake — where the thing Sofia described as a tree with bark of shadow is inside Callahan himself, and is eating twice as fast as it was three days ago.

She told them it went in through his mouth. She told them he has been screaming for months and nobody heard him. Those were close to the last words she had.

Thoril has half a life left and every hour of it is already promised to that door.

They are six.

They have never done this before and they have already done it once.
