Chaos in Wip

The morning they meant to leave, and nobody left.

Where we are: Innominandum is dead. The Feywild is whole. Fifty-nine hours stand between Tenebrosum and the end of Callahan’s seal, and the plan is simple — step into Alamil’s circle, come out on Mystica, ride to Albion, go down into the dark. Every part of that plan is about to stop being true.

What Cat said

The Hearring call, before anyone had finished waking up.

The message came through the Hearrings — the communication rings that work anywhere on the same plane — before anyone had finished waking up.

Catroniel, from Mystica, with two things.

The first: Thoril did not stay.

He landed on that continent and he kept going. He is already inside the Shadowfell — alone, at half of what he used to be, with the other half already promised to a door, and he is not answering anyone. He can feel his friend dying and he has decided he is not going to be too late twice.

Thoth, Kratos and Freya are still at the Edge of Existence. They will come to the Shadowfell the way they came to the Feywild — when Thoril calls them.

Nobody can reach him to make sure he does.

The second thing was smaller and stranger.

“Tell R4’s wizard to stop running tests.”

The circle

Ragar reads the circle. The Queen waits for the answer.

R4 went to find Alamil.

The room was empty. Nothing knocked over, nothing broken, nothing taken. A man who was there yesterday and is not there today.

So R4 got Ragar instead, and Ragar looked at the teleport circle for a while, and then said the thing nobody wanted:

It has been tampered with since it was made.

Not damaged. Not decayed. Altered. And whatever is written into it now, it will not put them where it says it will. He cannot tell them where it will put them.

He cannot fix it. He said so plainly, and that is where it was left.

Nobody has yet asked out loud whether “Alamil is gone” and “the circle is wrong” are two problems or one.

The Queen abdicates, sort of

The Outlands, after: one god, one empty field.

R4 is about to leave the mortal plane on a deadline he might not come back from, wearing a dead woman’s face, ruling a city that does not know it.

Luna Cantine came to find him.

On the correct morning, at the correct hour, in front of the one person who had a problem she happened to be the answer to. She did not ask for anything.

He made her intermediary Queen of Wip.

The Matriarch of the Wolves. Head of the largest lycanthrope family in the city, one of the three heads of the Thieves Guild, and the single most capable operator on the council. She took it.

Then he collected Una, used the Amulet of the Planes, and went back to the Outlands — to the field where the Dragonsmoot was abandoned mid-sentence.

There was nobody in it.

No dragons. No serpent gods. Not Bahamut, not Tiamat, not Kereska, not the serpent god Merrshaulk. Not Oman. Not Meiko. The greatest gathering of draconic power in ten thousand years had broken up and gone, and the god who called it was standing in an empty field by himself.

He did not say where anybody had gone.

R4 gave him a sending stone, to pass along to Oman, whenever IO manages to find him. IO took it. Nobody thought to ask a god standing alone in a field what had become of the assembly he called.

Then they came back.

The council

They convened in the War Room in The Rock. Rat attended from LIMA, listening in through a Hearring.

It went the way councils go. Grain, docks, guards, roads.

Two things in it mattered.

The first: the crater. Somebody had blown up the Catty Shack in the harbor district, and the council wanted an answer.

Luna gave them one. Far Realm creatures. In the city, in the night, doing what Far Realm creatures do.

Nobody at that table contradicted her. Including the Queen.

Far Realm creatures, said the Matriarch of the Wolves. Nobody at that table had a better idea.

The second: Mont Kiara.

Ulaxium of the Arcane University has stopped responding. He has not answered a message in some time. In his place, the council keeps receiving messages — from six strange people.

Who keep asking for “best friend.”

Smolder worked it out before anyone finished the sentence. Six people in one body is Ralf. And the best friend is him — he is the one who freed Ralf in this run, and it was not a small goodbye.

Luna asked whether he would answer the next one.

He said yes.

The next one has not come.

Rat goes to the Beastlands

Rat before the Cat Lord, Felina, and the weather that judges.

A panther walked into LIMA.

Felina. She had walked R4 and Sofia through the Beastlands in the summer. She had sat with Sofia while Sofia talked about trees. She had liked her.

She was there to summon Sofia to the Beastlands — the debt for the promise Sofia made in the summer, for the Fall, and could not keep, when the Cat Lord assembled the Mortai — the demigod-like cloud-beings of the Beastlands, the plane’s weather with a mind in it — on her word and was left standing in front of them.

Rat showed her Sofia.

Then he offered to go instead.

They took the offer.

He stood in front of the Cat Lord and the assembled Mortai for hours while they told him, in detail and at length and with no interruptions, precisely what had been done to them. He listened to all of it.

Then he gave them an apology. It was not a perfect one.

And then the Mortai moved on to the part of the rite that is not talking. The retribution nearly took him apart.

He came back standing. What the Beastlands have decided about their armies, nobody has said.

Steven finds her

The Everscale changes hands.

Word reached Steven that Sienna had been sighted, and he went, immediately, and did not stop.

Sienna is Essyliss.

They had it out. The vegetarian tenet, the Everscale, the argument at the Edge of Existence, all of it, in both directions, with Sofia lying a few feet away.

And then they mended it.

Not loudly. Nobody won it. Neither of them gave in, neither of them raised their voice, and at no point did a goddess descend or a prophet kneel. Two people who had hurt each other very badly said the true things out loud and were forgiven for them.

Both of them wrote a new tenet. Steven’s is:

“I am forgiven.”

Which is the only one of his ten that another person has to agree to. She agreed to it.

Hers is:

“I will not give up.”

Which is the only one of hers that nobody else gets a say in.

And she gave the Everscale back. Nine surviving tenets, live again, for the first time since the day he handed it over.

Then she told him the thing she had been carrying alone, the reason she was washing bandages in a volunteer house instead of standing with the World Serpent:

She thinks the fight against Ghroth is hopeless.

She had gone and looked at Mappo No Ryujin with her own eyes. It had very nearly killed her. She had drawn her conclusion and she had been living inside it ever since.

Steven told her about Innominandum.

Six people. A lieutenant of Ghroth, dead in its own engine. And him, standing in the room when it happened.

She changed her mind.

She agreed to help him. And she agreed to try to do something for Sofia.

And then

The whole city heard the question.

Steven and Una were walking back to The Rock when they saw them.

A flight of chromatic dragons, coming in low over Wip.

Then the sky went dark over the harbor and Tiamat landed on The Rock — on the building with the last Ley Crystal in the world inside it — and said, in a voice that every single person in that city heard at the same time:

“WHERE IS HE?!”

They talked to her. They got the shape of it.

She wants the Amulet of Null.

Not the leg. Not an apology. Not a favor. The mark on Rat was only ever how she found the thief; it was never the thing she was owed. She has come for it, and she is standing on the roof until she gets an answer.

And then she said the thing nobody expected her to volunteer. Her father sent her. IO dispatched her to find Null, and dispatched Bahamut to find Essyliss — the goddess Steven had found that same afternoon. She was not avenging a burglary. She was executing a commission, and she wanted them to know whose.

Here is the answer nobody wants to say out loud: Rat sent Carxi to take it. Carxi gave it to Mordenkainen, in Avernus, who is at this moment using it — with his own future self’s spellbook — to open a way into the Null Realm as an escape hatch if the war is lost.

Which risks the seal.

Tiamat is one of the three who set that seal, alongside Asgorath and Bahamut.

And the party is the only group of people in the cosmos who have already watched what happens when Null gets out: he rampaged, he killed gods at the Edge of Existence, and the war was lost.

They cannot explain how they know that.

Where that leaves them

They asked her for a moment to talk it over.

That moment is where we pick up.

⏱ Roughly forty-eight hours remain.

Not one of the hours they spent today moved them a single step closer to Albion. The circle does not work. Alamil is gone, and the one wizard left in the city has already said he cannot fix what was done to the circle. Thoril is already down there in the dark by himself, and the gods are waiting on a man nobody can reach.

There is a dragon god standing on their roof.

And Smolder still owes somebody a reply.
