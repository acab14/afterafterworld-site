The Dragonsmoot

Every dragon in creation, gathered in one place — and the day Una was forged in her father’s flame.

A raspy, straining voice had called to every dragon in the world, summoning them to the Outlands beyond Sigil: a Dragonsmoot, the first in ten thousand years, chromatic and metallic alike. The voice was IO — Asgorath, progenitor of dragonkind — and it came out broken and incomplete, because the force that should have made it whole was missing.

The party arrived (by way of Sylvania and Vessa) into a gathering on the edge of chaos: dragons brawling, serpent-gods arguing, ancient hatreds boiling over with nothing to hold them in check.

The first assembly of dragonkind in ten thousand years, and nobody had thought about the seating.

Una, forged

Then IO arrived, and the fighting stopped — and his mere presence triggered the Dracogenesis that had been building in Una for years. It stalled. She hung trapped between forms, neither woman nor dragon, unable to complete the change.

Kereska, dragon goddess of magic, recognized the ancient rite that could finish it: the Breath of Becoming. A dragon of Una’s own lineage had to breathe fire upon her. So Oman — her father — loosed his full flame, and Kereska wove draconic magic through the fire, and Una emerged from it a young golden dragon, forged in her father’s breath.

The Breath of Becoming: her father’s flame, Kereska’s magic, and the company that got her there.

It was the defining moment of the Dragonsmoot — and a quiet answer to the Field of Fire at Viavolca Way that had once rejected her. Fire had refused her mortal form. It accepted her draconic one.

The Field of Fire, years earlier. This is what being refused looked like.

She did not become a symbol by choice. But in a gathering where metallic and chromatic dragonkind could barely stand to share the air, both of IO’s great extensions reacted to her: Bahamut saw the metallic ideal made manifest, a golden dragon born of devotion and choice; Tiamat saw something far more unsettling — a dragon who had kept her mortal mind, who builds alliances and thinks in decades and cannot be predicted or controlled. Both now watch Una closely. What they will ask of her, no one yet knows.

IO’s purpose — and its problem

IO declared why he had called them all: to remake the World Serpent, the nine-fold draconic entity that was once whole, and unite dragonkind against Ghroth.

Every dragon in creation, holding perfectly still, listening to a plan that cannot currently be done.

But the World Serpent cannot be remade as things stand, and the gathering laid both reasons bare:

  • Essyliss is missing. She is the binding force — the connective tissue that lets the nine members cohere into one. Without her they can stand in the same place and still not become the Serpent. When Semuanya disparaged her, Steven rose to defend her publicly — and learned, for the first time, that the goddess he had served is a member of the World Serpent itself.
  • Null’s Amulet is gone. IO saw it: a flashback of Carxi slipping it out of Tiamat’s horde. And Tiamat caught the scent of the shadow mark on the severed leg in Rat’s bag and came surging toward the party — until IO intervened.

The interruption

IO charged the party with their next task: find Essyliss and bring her to the Outlands, so the Serpent can be made whole.

Then he began to say something about Null — and never finished. Mid-sentence, Thoth reached across the planes and snatched the party away, dropping them into the dying Feywild, where a Lieutenant of Ghroth was already pulling the plane apart.

What IO meant to say about Null remains unsaid.
