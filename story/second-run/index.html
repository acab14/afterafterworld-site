The Second Run Begins

New Game Plus: the same five years, played with foreknowledge.

The party plane-shifted back into Wip at the very start of the Five-Year Prep — but this time they remembered everything. Every alliance that came too late, every traitor they trusted too long, the one theft that doomed the whole world. This time they could move first.

Different choices

The changes started immediately. Where the First Run took years to flush out the shapeshifters, the party now exposed them on day one with the Boop of Truth, the shapeshifter-detection trick that had survived the time jump. Carxi was dispatched at once to take the Amulet of Null out of Tiamat’s horde, years before the First Run ever managed it. Alliances that once dragged on for years were sealed in months.

And Steven founded LIMA — his order of Essylissian volunteers — immediately after assassinating the shapeshifter posing as mayor of New Dwarf City.

The party splits — Year 1 Redux

With foreknowledge, the party stopped traveling as one cautious clump and split into a half-dozen simultaneous campaigns, each playing to a strength:

  • Asfache marched into the Land of Elements, united the fire clans, and earned the Field of Fire.
  • Una went to the Arcane University, befriended Meiko, and found her own father — the ancient gold dragon Oman — in the Djermani Hills.
  • Smolder descended into the Drow Underdark to play the long political game.
  • R4 built alliances across the mortal realm and beyond, ruling Wip behind the dead Emma Wip’s face.
  • Rat secured HIS Tower and sent Carxi to raid Tiamat’s Horde.
  • Sofia began awakening the travel trees that would one day let her teleport whole armies — and grow an Ent Army.

Between sessions: the summer in their own words

Much of that first summer was played out in writing, between sessions, in the campaign’s Discord. Two threads are worth keeping.

Rat and the Last Leg

Rat spent the back half of the summer on a question nobody else was asking: what time magic does to the dead. Then he bought a whistle from the Thieves Guild, sailed out onto open water, and blew it.

You blow the whistle on open water and Gamble Island slowly fades into view. You enter the rebuilt city, full of vice, ne’er-do-wells, and debauchery.

There was no memorial. The island had been rebuilt straight on top of its own wreck, and the people there only cared about the present moment. Rat’s own memory of the day it drowned, written by his player, is the best prose the campaign has produced:

Perched at 11 on the clock tower, the position Catroniel had assigned him, Radtismir focused his attention on the bustling street below. It was a familiar assignment, a familiar vantage point, a familiar con. Safe.

He blew. He didn’t hear his whistle, or the screams, or the cracking and crumbling of buildings. He heard only the crushing, consuming… condemning waves—and could only watch as the street below was swept away.

He found what he was looking for at The Last Leg Casino, Bar, and Grill, where the owner, a gnome named Gofor Hindle, had mounted a skeletal leg above the door as a mascot and a skeletal arm beside it, pointing the way in. “This way for good times.” The arm still wore Catroniel’s ring. Gofor offered to sell it for 250 gold.

“This way for good times.” The arm was still wearing Catroniel’s ring.

“Gofor,” Rat uttered—almost whispered, so quietly that Gofor need lean in to hear—”you know very well to whom that ring belongs. And its finger. Arm as well. You should remember that he trained me never to pay more than 5% of your first ask. I do.”

“You’ve got the damn ring, now go. You’re not welcome here, you rat.”

Rat paid nothing. The ring, it turned out, was a Ring of X-Ray Vision, and Gofor had never known. Rat put it on, whispered the word Cheshire, and saw a skeleton two feet under the casino’s front step, missing an arm and a leg.

Radtismir produced his R41F-Enhanced Pistol from its holster, whispering “Catroniel’s Vengeance,” and pouring a pouch of ball bearings into the barrel, on top of the loaded round.

Chaos. Radtismir’s paradise.

“You may leave.”

He took the leg down from the sign. “The Last Leg,” Rat muttered. He laid the bones on the casino’s own door, and then he did the thing he had spent a season learning to do.

The brittle skeleton trembles, each bone rattling with the stirrings of forgotten life. Time itself bends and spirals around the remains, reversing decay and weaving sinew, muscle, and flesh back onto the bones. Skin blossoms like petals. The body of Catroniel has been made whole, but remains lifeless.

The body needed a soul, and souls are the province of gods. Rat teleported to Steven, and appeared out of thin air in Thoril’s bar in the middle of a story about a dead demon god. Steven brought Catroniel back that night. The real one, this time.

Steven and the Lizard Kings

Steven’s summer went to the lizardfolk tribes who did not yet worship Essyliss. He presented himself as the DwarvoDraco, preached the future he had personally watched end, and brought culture: the New Dwarf City Dwarfway on tour, with Jersey Dwarfs, Dwarf Lightning, Chicago, and The Lizard of the Opera.

Each play is eye-opening to them, and they thoroughly enjoy the performances - especially The Lizard of the Opera. The main character is played by an actual lizard!

The Lizard of the Opera. The title role is played by an actual lizard, and the reviews were extraordinary.

The Lizard Kings were less moved.

You try to convince the Lizard Kings, but they are not having it. Seems they only follow one thing: Power. One by one you slay each king, resurrecting each in turn.

He left every king alive and in place, under his umbrella. By the end of summer, LIMA was as strong as it had been on the day the party first faced Ghroth. It had a rank ladder now, Lizard to Alligator to Dragon to Grand Lizard, and sending stones so it could run without him. Something else happened in those battles too, quietly, that he would not reckon with until a goddess made him.

You feel invigorated by the taste, but also feel your connection to Essyliss weaken. It’s hardly noticeable to you during the fighting, but you notice your tenet “I’m a Vegetarian” no longer does anything.

Planes the party has pinned

R4 spent the same weeks deliberately mis-aiming the Amulet of the Planes to see where it would fail him. Three new places answered, and the amulet remembers them.

Limbo — supreme chaos, a twisting, quicksilver place filled with bits and pieces of rocks, trees, the four elements, entire landscapes, strong winds, and random pockets of liquid, solid, or gas. Gravity operated only between solid objects that touched each other.

Pandemonium — All open space was filled with howling winds that carried the sound of every word, shout, or scream uttered in the place. Without adequate protection, beings with hearing were rendered temporarily deaf in a matter of seconds and permanently deaf in a few minutes.

Acheron — The land masses were blocks of geometric simplicity the size of nations floating in a sea of air. The blocks would slowly collide and part without causing any upheaval or tremor.

The doomsday clock still ticked. But for the first time, the party was ahead of it.
