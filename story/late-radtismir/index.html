The Late Radtismir

The funeral of a living man, and the war it paid for.

Where we are: Tiamat is standing on The Rock — on top of the last Ley Crystal in the world — demanding the Amulet of Null and asking for Rat by name. The party has asked her for a moment to confer. Roughly forty-eight hours remain on the Shadowfell clock, the teleport circles are sabotaged, and the road west now runs through people instead of doors. This is the moment they confer.

The promise

Una gave her the answer, speaking for all of them: we will hunt Rat down ourselves, and we will bring you the amulet.

They named a meeting place — Sylvania, the party city in the Outlands — and five heads weighed the offer and found it acceptable.

Then Tiamat left The Rock, and the city underneath it started breathing again — but just barely.

The exhale. A down payment, not a pardon.

Nobody said the other half of the plan out loud until she was gone.

The man who skipped his own funeral

First stop: the Beastlands, to tell Rat about his death.

He heard the whole plan out. And then he said he would not be coming to it.

Not out of fear — out of arithmetic. A senior Mortai attends Rat now: eight feet of slow grey weather with a face somewhere inside it, everywhere he goes, watching, saying nothing, reporting everything it sees to its seniors among the Mortai. It came back from the rite with him and it does not leave. Nobody stages the greatest fraud of their careers in front of the Beastlands’ idea of a court reporter.

So the con of the Second Run went forward without its master thief, on his own instruction — which may be the most professional thing he has ever done.

Rat stayed in the Beastlands, in front of the weather, doing nothing clever at all. Just showing it the things he actually holds to. Respect. Honesty. Honor.

Whether weather can be impressed, weather does not say. It looked impressed.

Rat and the weather. Nothing in his hands at all.

And before he left, the Cat Lord gave him two things. An answer: the beasts of the Beastlands are committed to the war at the Edge of Existence — and whether the Mortai come too, the weather will decide for itself. And a bell: small, heavy, and not to be rung until the time comes to fight at the Edge.

The funeral arrangements

Second stop: Avernus, and the two people best positioned to build a corpse.

Carxi heard the plan and grinned. Mordenkainen heard the plan, handed over the Amulet of Null, and got to work.

He built them a body. Rat, to the last scar, cold on a slab. And onto it went the thing that has been riding in the real Rat’s bag of holding since Sylvania: the severed leg — the one still carrying the chromatic mark, the mark Tiamat has followed across two planes.

The mark is how she finds her thief. From that moment on, her own tracking would swear the thief was exactly where the party said he was.

Mordenkainen builds a body; Carxi is having a lovely time. The leg is the part that sells it.

Before they left, they gave the archmage a second job: go to Wip and look at the broken teleport circles. Mordenkainen and Carxi went.

Limbo, and a stranger

Limbo. Where the ground does not agree with itself from one minute to the next, and where a falling rock needs no explanation.

That is where Rat died.

Crushed, tragically, mid-escape, fleeing from the party that had hunted him down, the stolen amulet still among his effects. A story with everything in it: a chase, a thief, justice, gravity.

Asfache and Smolder stayed with the corpse, keeping the scene honest, and a bit awkward.

And while they stood their strange vigil, they noticed they were being watched. Something in the churn was watching, and had been for a while. Asfache went after it. The chase ended the way Asfache’s chases end.

What he caught was a githzerai — gaunt, wrapped, entirely composed about being caught — whose first words were an allegiance check: did Asfache serve the githyanki, or the mind flayers?

Asfache told him the truth. He does not serve mind flayers. He kills them. He has split an elder brain in half with his own hands.

A warrior, a modron, and a githzerai who was supposed to be watching from a great deal further away.

The githzerai considered this. Then he gave his name — Dak’kon — and asked for their help. With what, he has not yet said. But he left something behind: a paper roll, instructions to his githzerai city, written in Deep Speech and Gith. An invitation, with an address, in two scripts nobody present could read.

He is, presumably, expecting them.

The long way to Sylvania

Meanwhile Una, R4 and Steven took the Amulet of the Planes and went to collect a dragon god.

The amulet had opinions. It put them in Fortitude, in the Outlands. Then it put them in a mirror dimension, where something gangly and slender and almost shaped like a man was present, and they did not linger. Then it put them on Pirate Isle, on the mortal plane, briefly and without explanation.

Then, at last, grudgingly: Sylvania, where Tiamat was waiting, as promised.

The handover

They led her to Limbo. To a rock, and to what was under it.

Five heads considered the scene, and every piece of it agreed with every other piece. The corpse with Rat’s face. The leg with her mark still burning in it. The Amulet of Null, recovered from a dead thief’s effects — exactly as promised, exactly on time.

She took it.

Corpse, mark, and amulet — every piece agreeing with every other piece. She left satisfied.

And then she looked at Una — the newest gold dragon in the world, the one who had made the promise and kept it to the letter — for slightly too long, and said:

“I’ll be keeping an eye on you.”

And then she was gone, her brother’s prison in her claws, believing Rat dead.

The most dangerous object in the cosmos, treated as a receipt.

Two verdicts in Wip

Back in Wip, Mordenkainen had an answer about the circles, and it was worse than the question.

They go to Avernus. All of them. Someone reworked the network so that every circle on the continent delivers its passengers to the Nine Hells — carefully, and deliberately. It is a trap, and it was built for this party.

He can fix it, he says. It would take him about a year. Without Alamil.

Against the clock they are living on, that is a no with extra steps. But it is the first time the door west has come with a price instead of a refusal.

And at LIMA, Steven looked in on Sofia — and found two goddesses arguing over her bed. Essyliss and Eldath, mid-disagreement, at whisper volume, about how to heal her. The idea on the table: forcefully wildshaping her. A new shape. A fresh body. Neither goddess would promise it brings her mind back.

Steven said he would talk to the party first. No decision has been made.

Six letters and a mountain

And then, at last, the message came for Smolder.

Six of them, actually. One from each of Ralf’s personalities, each contradicting the other five. Reading them in sequence was like hearing an argument through a wall.

So they went up Mont Kiara to have the argument in person — and found the Arcane University under attack. Devils and demons at the doors. And holding the doors against them: students. Dozens of students, moving with the same instincts, covering the same angles, because Ralf has been embedding his mind into the students and teachers.

Dozens of students, one set of instincts, and Ralf taking notes through all of it.

And in the middle of it all, Ralf. All six of him, in one body. Who finally explained what the letters could not agree on:

Caspian has made him an offer. A body for each personality. Six minds, six bodies, nobody sharing, ever again. Some of the Ralfs want it more than they have ever wanted anything. Some of them would rather die as one than live as six.

The contract arrives tomorrow.

The counteroffer

The party talked it over, and their position on the contract came together very fast:

If the counterparty — Caspian — is dead before the ink arrives, there is nothing to sign.

Ralf armed them himself — La Bombas, from the mountain Caspian has spent two years scheming to acquire, handed over by the wizard he is trying to buy, to the people now delivering them at speed.

Then a contact stone that had waited a very long time finally got used, and Malachas answered. The Arch Devil has wanted Caspian gone since the throne of Avernus was stolen out from under everyone. He did not need persuading. He brought forces. He brought intelligence. He found the target:

Caspian is aboard Zariel’s old flagship, flying over Avernus.

Mordenkainen teleported everybody to the war — everybody: with Tiamat well and truly gone, Rat rejoined his own “mourners” for the assault.

So that is what rose through the burning sky of the Nine Hells toward a warship: six adventurers — one of them officially deceased — one modron, one archmage, one extremely pleased thief, an Arch Devil, and his assembled host.

The ship opened fire.

Where that leaves them

That is where the session ended, and that is where the next one starts: mid-air, mid-barrage, over Avernus.

⏱ Roughly thirty hours remain.

The road west is open again, barely — an archmage who can carry them, an amulet that gambles — while the circle network stays dead until somebody spends a year on it, or rescues Alamil. Thoril is still down in the dark alone, and three gods still wait on a call only he can make. The contract still lands at Mont Kiara tomorrow. A githzerai’s scroll sits untranslated in somebody’s pack. A vote about Sofia is waiting for a quieter hour that may not come.

And somewhere in that boarding party, a bell in his pocket, the late Radtismir is very carefully staying dead in the middle of a war.
