Prologue — Before the Clock Started

Before there was a countdown, there was a tidal wave.

It began with a tidal wave. The party washed ashore from Gamble Island, half-drowned and a long way from anywhere, into the town of Saki — where they put down a nest of cultists and a demon before they’d even caught their breath.

In Saki they crossed paths with Baz Stricknaz: bastard prince of Jun, host to a Reaper, and a smiling recruiter looking for blades to add to his cause. The alliance was brief. When it soured, the party turned on him — and Bram put a knife to his throat. Baz died, and as he died he cursed. The Reaper woke, Baz’s shadow swallowed his own corpse, and what rose from it was a ghost-and-reaper hybrid consumed by rage. From that moment on, Baz has hunted Bram through the party’s shadows, relentless and patient. He only needs to catch him once.

Baz’s people outlived him. His half-siblings Willamet and Priscilla Adhmad, clerics who had wanted the ritual stopped as badly as anyone; Willamet’s bodyguard Mark; the spies Blade and Silrael; and six Junish soldiers all came with the party when it left Saki. Saki’s mayor had “perished in the battle,” and a man named Munyon, who had spent it saving townsfolk alongside the town’s mages, appointed himself the new one. His riders would join the muster later.

The siege of Wip

The party traveled inland to Wip with Baz’s people in tow and found the city already taken. Newport had sailed around the continent and come in from the north, and clockwork igloids under the commanders of the Melts family held every district but one: the fortress called The Rock, which had never been breached and was not going to start now.

What followed was the longest single stretch of the First Run. The three factions of the city’s Thieves Guild. A Rakshasa called Caspian, and the mage he took. The rescue of Luna Cantine. A gnome in a gun foundry who said he was Rat’s mentor. The fortress, the lab beneath it, a drill the size of a district, a drow spymaster with her own war, and a queen. It is told in two chapters:

→ 2.2: The Road to Wip — three days of training, two souls sold, and a city that had already fallen. → 2.3: The Rock — the fortress, the crystal, and the face R4 still wears.

It cost them. Ralf died in the Soul of the Rock and came back in an igloid body with Ley Crystal shards for eyes and his own hat on top. Emma Wip was murdered by the thing wearing Catroniel’s face. And in the chaos, R4 stepped into the dead ruler’s place, taking the reins of Wip’s government behind a borrowed face.

The clock starts ticking

Then came Jun, and the Battle of the Ziggurat.

Heart Melts — a clockwork dragon — crashed down through the temple floor and into the ziggurat below, and there he destroyed the Jun Ley Crystal. The barrier that had sealed that city against the dark went dark itself, and aberrant things spilled through.

The gods had built their wall out of crystal. Now one of its stones was shattered. Only the Wip Ley Crystal still held — and by every reckoning it had roughly five years of power left before Ghroth’s path to the world opened.

The doomsday clock had started ticking.

They killed him for it, at the Battle of the Jun Ziggurat. It did not put the crystal back.
