Farylse & the Chronologians

Time cops, a deal struck under duress, and a shattered plane where time is terrain.

You do not simply rewind five years of history without someone noticing.

The party’s jump was intercepted by the Chronologians — time cops, guardians of the timeline’s integrity. They fought their way through a Chronologian stronghold alongside Mordenkainen, freed Freya from captivity, and came at last before the Chronologians’ leader: Damian Heath.

The stronghold, as negotiated by Steven.

The deal

Damian did not want to stop them. He wanted to use them.

The terms were simple. He would release the party and Freya to go and defeat Ghroth. If they succeeded, he would recruit them to upset the balance of power within the Council of Eternity — the mysterious body that governs time itself. If they failed, they would die, and Damian would remain steward of a timeline already doomed to fall. He had nothing to lose by letting them try.

The party took the deal.

The shattered plane

Beyond Damian’s stronghold lay Farylse — a broken temporal plane where time isn’t a current you drift along but terrain you can climb, cross, and fall off of. The party spent what felt like a long while there, and met strange company:

  • the Unnumbered, exiles of the Land of Gears, cut off from their hive-mind when their king abandoned them;
  • the Void, and the Weavers who work the loom of time;
  • Captain Jhodos Rumoxath, a triton pirate king who gave everything to save his crew, and who taught the party to command Farylse’s temporal terrain;
  • a colossal Giant Robot washed ashore with a skeleton inside it;
  • and a crazed, long-haired woman in a crown, endlessly guarding a portal that led nowhere anyone could name.

The Land of Gears — a city on turning cog-plates, and the home the Unnumbered were cut loose from.

The Giant Robot, run aground and going green. Somebody had been inside it, and had not got out.

Not everything in Farylse wanted to teach them anything. The spider queen, for one.

And the Void, which is a place, and also — occasionally, and unpleasantly — a thing that comes out of it.

The things that live there

Not everything on Farylse was a monster. Some of what watched the gates had been people, once — people who made a deal with Damian Heath or with Astridia, and now keep the doors they bargained for.

Shatter. The gear-people’s souls, caught in time and still working. Each one is many of them at once, and they have never stopped digging.

Gloom. The immortal general of the City of the Palanquin, who carried a city and an army out of Ghroth’s path and then watched all of it turn into the other things on this page. Astridia offered to numb it if he would guard her portal. The party left with his Captain’s Seal.

Regret. Farylse’s name for Captain Jhodos Rumoxath, the triton pirate king who lost his crew and never once put it down. He taught the party to ride the time-terrain and gave them his Star-Shell Pendant.

They learned to manipulate the time-terrain, banking decades of it for later. And they tied up a loose end from the First Run: the White Lion had survived their ambush — Artists cannot truly be killed — and here he was again. The party returned his severed body parts (still tucked in their bags of holding) in exchange for a fresh writ to silence the Siren Song and two leonin followers, Nala and Raja.

When at last the way home opened, the party stepped through it knowing one hard rule: they can never return to Farylse. Whatever they carried out — items, allies, knowledge, the writ — was all they would ever get from that place.
