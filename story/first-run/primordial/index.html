The Primordial and the Time Cops

Mind flayers under a volcano, a goddess with a pair of scissors, and the first arrest in campaign history.

Year 2 opened with a stack of correspondence and one line of it worth remembering.

The pixies Sofia had been sending back and forth all winter finally returned from the Arcane University, and their report was short. The president is dead. Something has taken the school. And one of them had a message specifically for “best friend”:

“The president is dead. Long live the president.”

The rest of the pile was better news, mostly. The war in Gaiadon had been won by the Atrikans, on the strength of intelligence R4 had quietly handed them. The Melts had retaken Newport with Wip’s help and wanted trade. King Ramses wrote that Jun was thriving, and credited, of all things, the brothels. And spiders were pouring into Wip out of the sewers, which turned out to be Dryzzlazz — the Spider Queen of the Thieves Guild, gone completely insane, though it would be another year before anyone found out why.

Una got a letter back from her mother and learned that her father is not her father. Her real father is a shapeshifting gold dragon. She then found a book describing a process called Dracogenesis, by which certain people become dragons, and a reference to further reading — all of it, naturally, at the Arcane University that had just been taken over by something.

Steven was whisked away by Essyliss without explanation at the start of the season and reappeared at the end of it, covered in blood, in an extremely good mood about the number of converts he’d missed.

And Smolder, with both Smut Shaqs failing and a rival across the street, did what any reasonable entrepreneur does: he became a masked vigilante called Catman. Rat came along some nights. They tracked the robbers hitting the original Shaq to a warehouse belonging to Lord Wherebethal, and Smolder booked an appointment.

Then Felafel came round again — Smolder met Freya at his own festival and was granted the ability to grow his hair at will, Rat used the crowds as cover to steal three diamonds, and Una drank enough to briefly and accidentally visit the Astral Plane.

Viavolca Way

Ozai had been talking to the Long of his tribe, and the news from the Land of Elements was bad in a specific and alarming way. Something was attacking the lands around Viavolca Way: mind flayers, and things like them. And the mind flayers were hunting for tools that can weave time.

Asfache relayed that to the party, and the party went at once.

They led the charge with Ozai and the fire clans to the city of Volcarun and found it sealed inside a transparent purple-black wall. They hammered a gap in it; Asfache, Rat and Smolder got through before it closed and the rest fought their way in behind them. In the middle of the running battle, the Elder Brain spoke into their heads. It had noticed something in the party with the power to manipulate time.

It wanted to make a deal.

Beneath its lair, it said, was a primordial — sealed, ancient, and guarded by defenders it could not beat alone. Help it kill the guardians, hand over the time scroll, and it would take its people and leave the city.

The party agreed, descended into the tunnels, saw the Elder Brain, and immediately betrayed it.

Sofia and the elder brain. The green orb came out of one of these.

It had, of course, been planning the same thing, and the guardians and the elder brain dragons came down on them together. The party won. The Elder Brain fled into the Far Realm rather than die, exactly as Thoth had said it would. The bulettes and umber hulks it had been driving turned on the mind flayers, the survivors plane shifted out, and the wall around Volcarun fell.

Then the Aspect of Fate climbed out of Rat’s pack by itself, stopped being a die, became a glowing stone, and floated away down the tunnel.

Half the party went up to deal with the villagers — and to brutally end a rebellion Ozai had started in their absence. Rat went after the stone, chasing it down the tunnel it had drifted into, and half the party followed him down.

The scissors

At the bottom, in the dark, was a primordial, held fast in threads of time.

It offered a trade of its own. Free it, and it would tell them how to open the portals that Far Realm creatures escape through — the one thing the party had no answer to, and had already watched two of their enemies use.

Freya came out of the tattoo on Una’s back, took one look, and was extremely interested. She said something about having searched a great many timelines for this. Then she produced a pair of Temporal Scissors, which cut the fabric of time itself, and explained the plan: cut one strand of the primordial’s prison, let a little of it pour out, and crystallize what escapes. A Primordial Shadow. A key to Far Realm doors.

One strand, cut on purpose. Everything that went wrong afterwards is downstream of it.

She also mentioned, lightly, that the Chronologians were going to be unhappy about this.

There was an argument. It ended with Rat handing Freya the Aspect of Fate, and it is the single decision Thoth has never let him forget.

Freya cut one strand. The primordial began to pour out of the wound, violently, and the earth shook — and the time cops arrived, exactly on schedule, exactly as promised. Freya told the party to hold them off while she cast. They held them off. The spell landed, the shadow crystallized, and the first Primordial Shadow in the world existed.

Then the Chronologians took Freya and vanished.

What was left in the cave

The party rested where they stood, and their patrons came to them one by one.

Kratos gave Asfache The Chain — a weapon that adds another weapon’s damage to every attack it’s attached to. Thoth told Rat that getting the Aspect of Chaos from Thoril was now urgent, and told him what he thought of giving away Fate. The Oakfather taught Sofia how to grow a grove. And Essyliss took Steven to the Edge of Existence and made him look at it, and then repeated the three tasks in order, in case he had begun to think they were negotiable.

They woke up and Freya was standing there.

Different hair. Different clothes. She had escaped the Chronologians — and it had taken her centuries, from the inside, of being pursued through time. She had slipped them at last and come back to this moment on purpose. Finding the party had been easy: the Aspect of Fate points at them, wherever and whenever she goes.

She wanted the shadow tested immediately. They took it to the portal the Elder Brain had left and it worked — the door opened. Freya could not follow them through it. Something about the Far Realm, she said, rots divinity.

So the party went in without her and finished the Elder Brain in its own refuge.

Satisfied, Freya and Kratos went to work: they would sweep across the Land of Elements together, freeing the towns from the mind flayers and harvesting shadows of every primordial they could find. “We’re going to need as many as we can if we are able to defeat Ghroth.” Asfache put the fire clans under their orders. Smolder asked to be told when they reached the fire primordials.

The party went home through one of Sofia’s trees.
