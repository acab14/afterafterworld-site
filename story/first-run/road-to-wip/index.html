The Road to Wip

Three days of training, two souls sold, one city already lost.

They left Saki with more company than they had arrived with: Willamet and Priscilla Adhmad, clerics and half-siblings to the man the party had just killed; Mark, who goes where Willamet goes; the spies Blade and Silrael; six Junish soldiers; and Alissa the mage, who had the plan.

The plan was Baz’s plan with the human sacrifice taken out of it. Go to Wip, spend the King’s seal on magic items from Alissa’s contact, teleport to Jun, defend it. Everyone agreed it was a good plan. It was three days to Wip.

Three days of training

They used the road. Mark taught Asfache and Steven to parry; the clerics gave Smolder, Ralf and Bram a free spell a day each; Una studied with Alissa; and Blade and Silrael taught Rat how to hurt people who were not looking.

The road repaid the effort by trying to kill them roughly once a day. A kettle of migrating giant eagles came down on the column. A family with a broken cart axle came the other way with the first real news anyone had: Wip has been invaded. Then two glabrezus. Then two bone devils and six hellhounds, which is where the trip stopped being funny.

The devils offered safe passage in exchange for souls. Steven sold his. Ralf sold two.

Nobody made him. Ralf simply had more on hand than the others and saw no reason to leave a bargaining position unexploited. The party walked on unharmed and did not discuss it at length, which is a habit they have never broken.

Safe passage, priced in souls. Ralf paid double and has never explained why.

Goblins, WonderWads, and one exploding cart

After the devils came a strange little man named Tingle, who sold them maps of Junkweil, the world, and — repeatedly useful thereafter — the city of Wip, sewers included. Then two hundred goblin raiders out of the Vale ambushed them, and the party killed a hundred and forty-two and put the bodies in the bag of holding, because Steven wanted them.

Somewhere in there a merchant handed out free samples of WonderWads, which make you more charming. Everyone tried one. Ralf and Bram got hooked, and neither of them knew it — the party spent months noticing those two were slow off the mark in a fight and being unable to say why.

Then the traveling potion salesman they had wronged in a previous life came back for revenge. Una put him to sleep, somebody lifted a potion off his cart, and the Glyph of Warding on it detonated the entire inventory. Una’s tongue exploded. Asfache was teleported onto a stack of orange crates. Every woman present acquired cat eyes. Priscilla’s clothes changed color every time she cast a spell.

They arrived at Wip in that condition.

The silent city

Glassplace was empty. Not evacuated — silent. Doors shut, streets clean, nobody in them.

Ralf turned Rat and Smolder invisible and put them in the air, and the shape of it came clear: Newport had sailed around the continent and taken the city from the north.

Fishsong was thriving, its crowds listening to evangelists promise that under Lady Prodigy Melts they would be richer than ever — free WonderWads daily, an igloid for every family’s heavy lifting, a safe and better Wip — and offering a reward for anything useful about how to get inside The Rock. The Rock itself was ringed by igloids under a large man in a crown, Pyre Melts, with drones roped up its sheer sides swinging hammers. The Ghostforge was buried in fog that leaked blasts, flashes and the occasional scream. The Harbour was Newport war galleys, Westgate was igloids hauling the nobles’ furniture down to them, and Moongate was garrisoned.

And out in Traders Bay sat a minotaur ship, riding at anchor, waiting patiently to learn who it would be trading with.

Under the Glassplace

The company split. Priscilla, Blade and Silrael were flown invisibly in to find the Thieves Guild. Willamet, Mark and four soldiers went to Moongate, which went badly and would cost them.

On the ground, the party realized they were being watched by children. Una made friends with one — a halfling girl named Lyla — who showed them the way down into the underground city beneath Glassplace. There they met Liam, who wanted proof of who they were and knew where to get it: Otto the 7th, Bram’s brother, left behind by the same parents in the same way.

The brothers performed their famous knives act. Asfache won silver arm-wrestling. Smolder and Rat held a pickpocketing contest that Rat won and Smolder cheated at the end of, so it would look like he hadn’t. Both of them know what they know. Liam sold them the underground river and the sewer entrances for coin, a club, and every orange Asfache owned.

Stinky

The river ran ten feet wide and waist deep in the dark. Steven jumped in first. It turns out Steven is a phenomenal swimmer.

Rat walked into a giant snake. Asfache fed it WonderWads, noticed it was guarding a clutch of eggs, and got everyone past without a scratch. He named it Stinky. Smolder gave serious, extended thought to stealing an egg and — this is worth recording — did not.

Further on the stalactites dropped on them. Darkmantles, swarming, wrapping heads in magical darkness, with giant centipedes coming up the tunnel behind. Una cleaned the water with a cantrip so they could see what was biting their legs. Smolder netted one, skewered several more, and kept one as a hat, to mirror Ralf’s.

The underground river. Smolder kept one as a hat.

The DwarvoDraco

Then Steven went for a swim.

He had spotted alligators, and the alligators liked him, so he went deeper — and found a tunnel with a stone lizard head carved above it. The symbol of Essyliss. Behind it was a mushroom-lit cavern and ten lizardfolk who worship her, farming glowing fungus and stealing the occasional crate of fruit from the neighbors without ever hurting anyone. Steven joined their prayer — “I’m a lizard” — and their elder, Aussssten, asked him one question: are you traveling with a dwarf?

He was. He fetched the party, and when Aussssten saw Ralf beside him in a wizard hat riddled with stars, that settled it. On the cavern wall was a mural: a lizard head wearing a nose ring, and above it a dwarf surrounded by stars. Steven was the DwarvoDraco — he who walks with dwarves — and had apparently been so for some time before anybody thought to mention it.

Aussssten gave him the Everscale — the totem of the chosen of Essyliss, which lets its bearer put a point of magic into anything they make with their own hands, once every six days. And wielded by the DwarvoDraco, said Aussssten, it is what restores Essyliss to the power she held before the great destruction.

Ralf and Smolder spent the next hour agreeing that Steven had absolutely not told them everything, and that these lizard people were ridiculous. They were right about the first part. They have been wrong about the second ever since.

“Those are my fighters!”

The sewers surfaced through a grate into a fighting arena — the headquarters of the Wip Thieves Guild.

Smolder went up first and was met by Dryzzlazz Dig’Rodar, the Spider Queen, a drow drider, who asked whether they had anyone to vouch for them and started dropping her children on them when they didn’t. Priscilla, from the crowd: “STOP! Those are my fighters!” Dryzzlazz took it in stride and asked who was fighting today.

Asfache volunteered, then raised the stakes to a death match, which the crowd loved. He won brutally, looked to Ralf for a ruling on whether to finish it, got one, and finished it. Afterward he made friends with Skoll Cantine, son of the matriarch of the Wolves, and his wolf Lucas. Una learned the leader of the Moles was missing and something had gone wrong in the Ghostforge, and Bram quietly handed Smolder a token and a letter he had lifted during a reverse heist with Otto.

The Big Meet

Three heads run the Guild: the Spiders (Dryzzlazz), the Wolves (the Cantines, Skoll speaking), and the Moles — Aegor, who did not attend and was never going to.

The news at the table was ugly. Willamet had been captured, having opened his mouth in Moongate, and the Cantines meant to trade him to Newport for their kidnapped matriarch, Luna Cantine. Skoll’s terms were simple: rescue his mother before the trade, and he would hand Willamet back.

Then Smolder “happened” to find a third token. Dryzzlazz dropped it in the Ghostblood chalice, which confirmed what had become of Aegor and expanded a sixty-foot Zone of Truth over the meeting. What followed was a masterclass in telling the truth without saying anything, capped by Rat performing a piece of sleight of hand that duplicated a ten-thousand-gold writ of Jun in front of a room full of professional thieves. The deal: passage to the Harbour, in exchange for solving the Ghostforge problem, protecting the prisoner exchange, and ten thousand gold. Priscilla paid half up front. Dryzzlazz passed the writ to Skoll and told him to cash it quickly — the things were getting harder and harder to cash.

Afterward Rat got a hand on Dryzzlazz and spoke to her in touch-based thieves’ cant. She was being controlled by arachnomancers, she told him, who kept her true and insane personality suppressed, and who meant to steal guns and take them down to the drow.

The Harbour

The Guild moved them by sewer toward the shortest crossing, at Shady Lane. They were caught anyway, brawled with igloids, started losing, and escaped underground on a distraction improvised by Priscilla and Alissa.

So they rode the underground river instead, surfaced in the bay, and Rat and Smolder set Newport ships alight as a distraction. By the light of the burning galleys they crossed the rooftops to the Giddyup Saloon, where Alissa finally admitted what her contact was. She had signed a contract with a fiend: information from her, magic items for her allies, a Ring of Mass Teleport for herself.

Inside, two men flanked a four-foot mirror. A voice welcomed her and told her to take her ring and look into it. She looked into it, went white, and was gone.

The Giddyup Saloon. Alissa looked into the mirror, and was gone.

Caspian

The mirror’s owner introduced himself as Caspian. He was a rakshasa, he was unhurried, and he had no intention of giving her back — he needed a student of the Arcane University to open the enclave at Mont Kiara, and Alissa was one.

They attuned their new gear and went back in. Smolder, in his Robe of Eyes, listened in while Caspian met Prodigy Melts and traded 150 igloids for the Magic Bomb spell and whatever he could learn about the Ghostforge. Prodigy mentioned an exchange she had to make later that day; she thought it might get her the key to Jun. Meanwhile Rat took three thousand gold in gems and coin out of the building, and then found a bag with a thousand more in platinum.

Confronting Caspian got them nowhere — he would simply planeshift away if pressed — but he let them speak to Alissa through the glass. She blurted out the location and passphrase of the enclave, believing it discharged her contract, and Caspian pointed out, pleasantly, that the wording was give me what I need to reach the enclave, and only a student’s hand would do that.

So Alissa made her own terms: she would go with him, if he gave the party a Ring of Mass Teleport. And she made them swear they would save Jun.

Luna, and the bite

Smolder’s eavesdropping had also produced Luna Cantine’s location: a ship in the harbour called the Gentle Perfection.

Rat ran the extraction, and it was clean. They teleported into the Thieves Guild with the matriarch of the Wolves in tow, hours before Newport expected to be handed her. Skoll returned Willamet to Priscilla on the spot, granted free passage through Moongate, and offered Asfache the bite. He took it, and the Cantines made him a lycanthrope.

Luna gave them the one thing the Guild had never managed to buy: if you want the ghosts out of the Ghostforge, the answer is at the Heartforge.

The Ghostforge

They teleported into fog. Igloids and freed ghosts were fighting each other in it.

They fought ghosts, chased down a gnome named Gandor, and then a teenage human burst into the room, cracked his voice, tripped, and farted. This was Perception Melts, and he was delighted to meet them, and he believed every word when they told him they were ghostbusters from NDC. He offered three thousand gold for a clean removal. The next day he and Prodigy turned up ready to storm the district alongside them, and Una talked them out of it on the grounds that professionals work alone. Prodigy left saying she would like a word with Smolder later, about their mutual friend.

Gandor took them to the Heartforge, and in the Heartforge was a gun manufacturing line, and at the head of it was a gnome with an artificial arm and leg.

Catroniel. Cat. Rat’s long-lost mentor.

It was a very big moment. Cat had killed Aegor and had been building guns in secret with his crew, the Ghostbloods — gnomes, dwarves, and his lieutenant Carxi. His ambition was straightforward and enormous: take the third seat on the Guild, unite the factions, smuggle a force into The Rock, take it, and throw Newport out of Wip. He also knew about a minotaur smuggling operation running guns from Gaiadon into The Rock, and when. The party told him Tamsend came first. Cat said he would meet the Guild without them.

Meanwhile, Bram

Bram and Otto had been separated from the party at the saloon, and spent four days having an entirely different campaign.

They fled into the alleys, got lost, and pulled a gnome woman in black out of the hands of igloid brawlers — Carxi, who knew the city and had a rowboat. They rowed for the Ghostforge shore under fire and were saved by a minotaur ship out of Gaiadon, whose crew Bram talked into letting them perform. From that performance he learned about a shipment of power armor and guns going into The Rock in four days, and the meeting point: an ash tree in Cleargate Fields. Then he imitated the captain’s voice well enough to get a grunt to hand over the passphrase — “Rock Strong Keepers Brave” — and told nobody, because it was the only thing he had to bargain with.

Carxi took them to Catroniel, who was unimpressed by the withholding and visibly respected it anyway. The brothers helped run a trial of the smuggling route and were paid in exactly one gun. Then Bram spotted someone in the bushes and pulled out Lord Franklin Wherebethal, a merchant hiding in the woods, who took them to Farmans Grotto — a secret hollow full of Wip’s displaced nobility. Two nights of entertaining nobles bought them the state of the city and Sammy, a dwarf leader out of Cleargate who offered fighters in exchange for weapons. On the fourth day they collected their pistols, five bullets apiece, ammunition made of platinum.

Then they led Cat, Carxi and a squad of Ghostbloods through the sewers to the arena — because the Guild had told Cat to prove himself by finding it, and the Ash brothers were the only people he had who already knew where it was.

Mont Kiara

Cat’s meeting worked. He became the third head of the Thieves Guild.

The party missed it. They were at Tamsend Arcane University, following Ralf’s lead, which meant visitor passes and a call on his old conjuration professor, Dr. Fando Elzir. They bought an owlbear mascot plushie at the gift shop on Ralf’s tab, drank experimental potions in Research and Development, bought scrolls, and paid for a teleport to Mont Kiara — which put them on the summit, which is the wrong end of the mountain.

Ralf got them down with Tenser’s Floating Disk and Feather Fall, faked moonlight with an illusion so the door would open, and sent Merlin ahead as bait. At the far end of the enclave they found Caspian, and beside him a silent, blank-faced Alissa under his control. Rat and Steven put her down without hurting her, and Rat lifted the seal for the writs off her while she was out.

Then Asfache killed Caspian with one enormous, pent-up critical hit.

They swept every paper in the room into a bag — including an incomplete Magic Bomb spell — picked up Alissa, put on her Ring of Mass Teleport, and went home to a sewer in Wip, where they made a hut and laughed until they couldn’t breathe.

Cat was meeting the Thieves Guild that evening. He had a plan for The Rock, and he wanted Rat to run part of it.

→ 2.3: The Rock — a fortress that had never been breached, and everyone in the city with a plan to be inside it.
