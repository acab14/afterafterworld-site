HIS Tower

A devil in a door, a lich’s library, and the worst decision of Year 1.

HIS Swamp sits on the western side of Junkweil, and HIS Tower stands in the middle of it, and around the tower is an undead horde deep enough that Carxi had taken one look and gone home for reinforcements.

She got them. The Thieves Guild and the forces of Wip went into the swamp together in the winter of Year 1, and while a full battle raged around him, Rat knelt at the tower door and picked an arcane lock.

Down the tower

Inside, a large and deeply unpleasant undead thing was waiting, and lost.

Behind it was a puzzle room, and behind the puzzle room was a great deal of shouting. The party tried everything. The answer, arrived at eventually and by methods best not examined, was:

“Bing bongabong boing boop boop boop.”

“Rat with Malacus?” — a misheard Arch Devil, immortalized.

The next room held a door, and inside the door was an Arch Fiend.

Malachas had been sealed in it for a very long time and wanted out, and he was willing to trade. He knew where the library was. The party knew how to open a door. The deal they struck was careful and, in hindsight, extremely cheap: they would free him, and in exchange he would leave them a means of contact and answer any question they asked him truthfully, for two weeks.

They freed him. He vanished immediately — with, he noted, two weeks and one minute of grace before the contact stone was due.

The door he had been holding shut opened onto a horde of skeletons and banshees, which the party looked at, counted, and declined. They got on a flying carpet and sailed straight over the top of it into a portal. Then a Death Tyrant, then more portals, and then, at last, the place Thoth had sent them for.

Thoth’s last word before they crossed the threshold: once you are inside, my power cannot reach you.

The mistake

HIS Library held a mystical orb, suspended over a table carved with arcane runes.

It also held Prodigy Melts, who explained that she was there looking for a way to wake the inert warforged, and that the key to it was whatever was sealed inside the orb.

The party had been dealing with shapeshifters all year. They were suspicious of her. They discussed it. And then they let her carry on.

That was the mistake.

Whatever was sealed in the orb was, in fairness, exactly what she said it was.

What came out of the orb was Nyarlathotep — an agent of Ghroth, older than the current arrangement of the world and beholden to none of it. Ink flooded the library, and out of the ink came replicas of every member of the party. And in the same moment Prodigy dropped the face she was wearing: she was Nephren-Ka, a shapeshifting servant of Ghroth, and she had been standing next to them the whole time.

The thing that had been standing next to them all afternoon.

They fought their own shadows, and a shapeshifter horde, and Nephren-Ka, in a room where their god could not hear them. They won. It did not feel like winning. Nephren-Ka was not destroyed — she retreated into the Far Realm and left the trace of a portal behind her — and Nyarlathotep slipped away after her, to somewhere nobody could name.

Outside, the battle for the swamp had been won. Rat found Carxi and she began giving her report.

Then Carxi walked up.

There were two of them. Both were chained and marched back into the library while the party worked out what to do about it.

What Thoth had to say

With Nyarlathotep gone, Thoth’s power reached them again, which told them something useful about what had been blocking it.

He told them a rule about their enemy, and it has held ever since:

“The more powerful of Ghroth’s ilk will travel to the Far Realms to escape danger. They almost always strike again.”

Then he told them a story.

Long ago, The Great HE captured Nyarlathotep, and used its power to split the divine power of Truth into two material aspects: Chaos and Order. Several gods, Thoth among them, fell on him at once. In the fighting the gods seized Chaos and Order — but so much raw divinity and arcana was bleeding out of the fight that it made a third aspect nobody had intended: Fate. The Great HE used Nyarlathotep’s god-repelling power to drive the gods off, took Fate for himself, and conquered the world with it. Adventurers eventually killed him, wielding Chaos. How they came by it, nobody knows.

Thoth then looked at the runed table in the middle of the library and told them what it was.

Rat put out his hand.

Rat, as the dice see him.

The table became a six-sided die.

Thoth laid out the rest of it. Chaos is held by Thoril, a god fallen from power, cast down by some magic or other, last heard of somewhere in the mountains of Onkro. Order is held by Tyr, who is fighting at the Edge of Existence. And bound together the right way —

“Binding the aspects of Chaos and Fate through Order itself creates what we seek — Truth. We can use that to identify the servants of Ghroth.”

Which was, at that moment, the single most valuable thing in the world. The party had spent a year discovering that they could not tell who anyone was.

The rest of the tower

Thoth left. The Carxi problem remained, and the spell to solve it needed a day to prepare, so they spent the day robbing a lich.

They tricked and killed the automaton in the control room. They sent the skeleton army into the Maze of 1000 Deaths ahead of them and then looted the maze anyway, coming out with Bloodthirst — Swordy, to Smolder — a vorpal longsword with opinions — a set of metal casts for making phylactery pieces, a celestial pokeball, and a scroll nobody had a use for. They failed to open two chests. Smolder murdered an entire menagerie. Rat found books on poison, and a book on how to build a phylactery and become a lich, and took both. Una named her falcon Etta, short for Shrubetta.

And they found the scrying room, and finally saw the other end of the eyes that had been on them all year: wizards, prisoners, in a dark tower in Hell.

They tried very hard to open Nephren-Ka’s portal, and could not. She opened it herself the next morning, walked back in, and was driven off into the Far Realm a second time.

Then the spell was cast on the two Carxis, and it did not work.

So they killed both of them, and resurrected the one they wanted.

It was the correct call and it cost them anyway. The imposter died. The real Carxi came back, and she came back knowing exactly what her friends had decided about her, and it took a year and a half to fix.

The scroll from the maze went into a bag and was forgotten about. It was a Time Travel Scroll. Nobody had a use for it for two more years.
