The Dream and the Long Preparation

Seven gods, seven assignments, and a year spent building a machine nobody had blueprints for.

They slept, and the gods came all at once.

Not a vision each, politely scheduled. One dream, everybody in it, the whole pantheon standing around the party like a committee that had run out of options — which, it turned out, is exactly what they were.

What Essyliss said

Essyliss did the talking first, and she was brutally clear. The danger of Ghroth is three-fold, and it has to be answered in order.

He has a horde. The lesser things of the Far Realm can already reach the mortal plane; only his major forces are still held back by the last Ley Crystal. Deal with what is already here. Raise an army for what is coming.

When the crystal fails and Ghroth arrives, he will sing his Siren Song — and if the song is sung uncontested, it opens a road for the Great Old Ones to find this universe and eat it. Find a way to silence the song.

And when the song is silenced, that will not be a victory. It will be a fair fight. Find a way to beat him, or drive him out again.

What everybody else said

Freya closed off the easy answer before anyone could reach for it. The Ley Crystals cannot be restored. They were made from the energy released when the world dragon was split, and nothing short of a comparable cosmic wound will refill them. Then she turned to Una and Smolder and asked them to spread magic and put new life into the world, because her power grows with it.

Thoth told Rat about a great lich of an older era who had held forbidden knowledge concerning Fate and Chaos, and whose library still stood in HIS Swamp, on the western side of Junkweil. He wanted it found. He also asked Rat to spread truth in all its forms — and taught him a ritual to make contact.

Kratos did not ask. He simply took Asfache to Valhalla, ran him through a series of physical trials, watched him pass all of them, and named him Champion. Then he pointed him at the warriors of Viavolca Way and told him to go and become their leader, and to find glorious battle wherever it was hiding.

Kratos did not ask. He watched, he approved, and then he pointed.

The Oakfather and his three daughters — Eldath, Mielikki, Shiallia — gave Sofia a bag of seeds with pieces of their divinity in them, and asked her to plant them in new lands.

A goddess who did not yet give R4 her name told him she could feel the lost souls of the inert warforged: in Jun, and more in Newport, Halos, Holoc and Mopak. She asked him to wake them.

Väinämöinen sang, and told Bram to come and find him in Yore. He knew a way to silence the Siren Song.

Then the gods explained why they were delegating the end of the world to eight mortals and a warforged: they were already fighting. All of them, at the Edge of Existence, against everything of Ghroth’s that had slipped past the crystals. The gods were not coming to help. This was the help.

Smolder missed the end of the briefing. He was yanked out of the dream by a demon attacking him in his sleep, which was, in fairness, on brand for the entire five years that followed.

And Ralf, who had a fistful of Ley Crystal shards embedded in his skull courtesy of Smolder, discovered he could use them to summon a body made of raw arcane energy. Nobody asked him to. He simply could.

The machine

What followed was a year of the least glamorous work the campaign has ever produced, and it is the reason any of the rest was possible.

R4 started collecting world leaders the way other people collect debts. He opened trade with Newport — airships, guns, and every inert warforged in the city, in exchange for helping the Melts family rebuild the place they had just finished losing. He shipped the dormant warforged of Junkweil to Wip by the boatload. He sent advisors to Onkro, opened the southern ports, and founded the WANC — the Wip Associated New Commonwealth, Wip’s own council, as distinct from the Council of United Members that speaks for the whole home archipelago — which is a genuinely terrible name for what became the spine of the alliance.

Alamil began laying teleport circles: Wip, then Jun, then Halos, one a season, which is roughly four times the rate the art is supposed to allow.

Ragar began building La Bombas. By the end of summer he had nine, made from precious gems, each holding a little over a hundred magic missiles. Then Rat handed him shards of the shattered Jun Ley Crystal to experiment with, and the numbers stopped being reasonable: a Ley Crystal receptacle holds nine hundred. Ragar spent the rest of the year quietly filling one.

Rat bought books by the crate and turned the guild’s thieves and tinkers into a procurement service for poison research, then had the final volume stolen out from under him by devils working demons — which was the first thread of a much longer story, and he pulled it. Carxi went into HIS Swamp to scout, and came back saying she needed an army.

Una went to Jun and then Halos, rebuilding and spreading Freya’s name, and heard two things worth keeping: that there is a dragon god called Kereska, and that an ancient gold dragon named Meiko lives at the base of Mont Kiara, at the Arcane University.

Steven walked south through the forests and the Forbidden Jungle, converting anything that would hold still, and kept going.

Asfache and Sofia took ship for Viavolca Way and spent a year on the fire clans. Asfache challenged the leader of the Kai Clan, Momoa, to an Agni Kai, and won it. The clans were less impressed than he’d hoped — only the Flavor Clan under Fieri Yug signed on — but he left Ozai in place as his right hand with orders to have an army ready, and burned the last charge of a ring of mass teleport getting home.

Smolder failed to grow his hair back, meditated on the failure, and invented a festival about it. The Festival of the Lizard — Felafel — was a wild success: new life in the world, Freya’s favor, converts for Essyliss, and an enormous amount of free advertising for Smolder’s Smut Shaq.

Bram took his entire family to Glarsht and found Väinämöinen, who could not speak and therefore explained everything through mime. The gist: a powerful creature called the Lion Who Shakes the Spear had taken his voice. He guided the Ashes to a holy site, and there they found a map.

Three warnings nobody could act on

Three things arrived that year that the party could not do anything about, and all three came back.

A distress call from Ulaxium, president of the Arcane University at Tamsend: something has invaded. R4 sent Skol Cantine with seven La Bombas to investigate. Skol went silent, and stayed silent, and Luna Cantine spent the winter being told to wait.

At Orefnan, Steven and Una walked into a war between the city and the Fey and got flatly contradictory accounts of who started it. What they learned underneath the argument mattered more: the Feywild is collapsing. The Fey need it fixed or they need somewhere else to live. Una and Steven made the trade with the fey emissary Aquandria — we will save the Feywild, and in five years you will stand with us against Ghroth — and brokered a ceasefire to hold it together.

And Smolder noticed, through his Robe of Eyes, that he was being scryed on. He went to Rat and found that Rat was too. Somebody was watching the party, patiently, and had been for a while. Smolder’s response was to write a note to whoever it was and leave it out to be read. Shortly afterward, tieflings sabotaged the Smut Shaq, and a rival brothel called the Catty Shack opened directly across the street.

By winter, Rat had sent word for everyone to come home. The whole party met in the war room in The Rock to plan an invasion of a lich’s tower — and as they walked out of the building, Smolder looked up and saw the scrying orbs. One over every member of the party.

Two over himself.

One orb each. Two for the one who noticed.

→ 2.5: HIS Tower — into the swamp, and the worst decision of Year 1.
